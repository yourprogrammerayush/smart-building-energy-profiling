Problem Statement & Research Design

Five Concrete Research Questions Answered End-to-End

Commercial office towers and university campuses routinely waste 15–30% of purchased electricity through rigid HVAC schedules, stuck economizer dampers, and overnight baseload drift. Every method in this study maps strictly to the official IILM University Data Visualization and Analysis (26CSEM321) 6-unit syllabus.

RQ1 · Temporal Wastage

When is electricity wasted?

Off-hours (22:00–05:59) average 591.8 kWh/h—retaining 59.5% of the weekday work-hour mean load (994.6 kWh/h) and consuming 25.30% of total electricity (3,460.7 MWh) when buildings sit unoccupied.

RQ2 · Thermal Coupling

How does weather drive load?

Outdoor dry-bulb temperature exhibits a strong linear correlation (r = 0.750, p < 0.0001) above the 18.3 °C cooling balance point, adding +515 kWh/day per CDD on weekdays.

RQ3 · Expected Demand

Can we predict healthy load?

Unit II Multiple Linear Regression achieves R² = 0.9351 (forecasting) and R² = 0.9000 (counterfactual weather-schedule baseline without lag masking) on 8,760 out-of-sample 2017 hours.

RQ4 · Anomaly Profiling

Which days exhibit waste?

Unit IV One-Class SVM (F1 = 0.8079) and K-Means (k = 4, Sil = 0.380) isolate 157 anomalous wastage days led by 2017-04-26 (+2,972.3 kWh excess).

RQ5 · Actionable Roadmap

How do we cut 15–20% cost?

Four targeted BMS interventions above a protected 360 kWh 24/7 critical floor save 1,265,123 kWh/yr (18.50%), worth $151,815/yr and 527.6 tCO2/yr.
